# Public Review Guide: Sales Director 010

This guide is challenge-specific but intentionally high level. Read [SCORING.md](../../SCORING.md) first: the five evaluation dimensions, the evidence tiers, and the number source labels apply to every challenge. Private reviewer calibration, answer benchmarks, and follow-up exercises are not published.

## What Strong Submissions Demonstrate

### 1. A real thesis on selling two motions at once
The company sells services and software-assisted products, which have different buyers, cycles, proof, and pricing logic. Strong submissions articulate how the pitch, process, and pipeline differ per motion and where the transition story is hard, drawing on comparable complexity they have actually sold through.

### 2. Player-coach math that adds up
Carrying a personal target while building a team is a time-allocation problem. Strong answers show the split, the rules for when the director joins a deal versus coaches, and how they prevent themselves from becoming the bottleneck. Vague "lead by example" language does not survive review.

### 3. Public-source specificity about this company
The brief requires named, source-labeled specifics about the company's market position and competitors from public research. Strong submissions demonstrate they did the homework; the auto-reject list explicitly includes answers that could apply to any agency.

### 4. Honest scaffolding of assumptions
Actual targets are shared privately, so the entire plan rests on labeled assumptions for targets, coverage, cycle length, and conversion. Strong plans state each assumption, show the ramp math built on it, and flag which assumptions the plan is most sensitive to.

### 5. An outbound machine with quality control and ethics
The prospecting play should be executable immediately: list logic, personalization approach, follow-up structure, QA gates, and handoff, with clear guardrails against spammy or deceptive automation. AI should appear as a working system, not a tools list.

### 6. An artifact a sales leader would actually run
Pipeline review templates, forecast models, coaching cadences, or dashboards with labeled inputs. The strongest artifacts show operating discipline: what is reviewed weekly, by whom, and what decision each number drives.

## Challenge-Specific Failure Modes

- **Generic revenue leadership.** Frameworks and slogans transplantable to any company, the first item on the brief's auto-reject list.
- **Unlabeled ramp math.** A 90-day plan whose numbers have no observed/estimated/benchmarked/assumed labels, another explicit auto-reject.
- **One-motion thinking.** Treating the services retainer and the product sale as the same deal with different price tags.
- **AI theater.** Naming tools without a before/after, a QA step, or any evidence of a workflow you actually ran.

## Evidence That Matters for This Brief

- **Tier 2** is the floor: the operating artifact itself — forecast model, cadence doc, sequence design — inspectable with labeled inputs.
- **Tier 3** strengthens it: sanitized records from your real selling and coaching work (pipeline snapshots, sequences, call plans, automation traces).
- **Tier 4-5** is the differentiator: measured before/after from a sales workflow you automated or a team you ramped, with method and a confirmable source.
- Keep the brief's given facts, your public research (cite it), and your assumptions clearly separated in the evidence log. Reviewers check the public claims.

Strong or close submissions may be asked to defend the ramp math live with one assumption changed.
