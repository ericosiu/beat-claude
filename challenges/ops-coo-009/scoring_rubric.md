# Public Review Guide: Ops COO 009

This guide is challenge-specific but intentionally high level. Read [SCORING.md](../../SCORING.md) first: the five evaluation dimensions, the evidence tiers, and the number source labels apply to every challenge. Private reviewer calibration, answer benchmarks, and follow-up exercises are not published.

## What Strong Submissions Demonstrate

### 1. Triage that acts before it fully understands
The first-48-hours section reveals operating instinct. Strong answers show a concrete sequence: how they find the riskiest aging items fast, what they stabilize immediately, what they deliberately defer, and what single working session with the sponsor must produce. The brief explicitly penalizes plans that begin with a listening tour.

### 2. A risk-tiering system that is operational, not conceptual
Strong submissions show the actual decision tree or criteria: what auto-executes, what an operator reviews, what escalates, with examples per tier. They also answer the hard question the brief plants — what happens when something auto-approved should not have passed — with a detection and recovery path, not a shrug.

### 3. An audit with kills in it
The workflow table exists to force portfolio judgment. Strong answers apply a visible framework, retire or merge some workflows, and justify the keeps by expected outcome and ownership, not sentiment. Keeping everything and adding process is a named losing pattern.

### 4. Personal AI operating evidence
The brief asks for a workflow you personally automated, with before/after and labeled numbers, and for how you validate an automated recommendation outside your own expertise. Strong answers are specific enough to verify; vague fluency claims stand out immediately here.

### 5. Cadence installation from lived experience
Anyone can describe a meeting rhythm. Strong submissions recount installing one where none existed: the mechanism, how resistance was handled, and how follow-through was checked rather than assumed.

### 6. Self-awareness with real texture
The working-style section rewards honest, specific reflection, including a real example of adjusting to feedback. Polished self-praise reads as generic; named weaknesses with guardrails read as senior.

## Challenge-Specific Failure Modes

- **Meeting-first triage.** A 48-hour plan that is entirely discovery conversations, deferring every decision. The brief calls this out directly.
- **The conceptual tier chart.** Green/yellow/red defined by adjectives instead of criteria, fields, thresholds, or examples a team could apply tomorrow.
- **Additive bureaucracy.** An operating system that increases total review load instead of reducing it, defeating the stated purpose.
- **Anonymous operations experience.** Stories that could belong to anyone, with no mechanism, no numbers, no verifiable residue.

## Evidence That Matters for This Brief

- **Tier 2** is the floor: the decision tree, cadence design, audit framework, or dashboard itself, inspectable.
- **Tier 3** strengthens it: sanitized records from operating systems you actually ran — real cadence docs, real decision logs, real automation traces.
- **Tier 4-5** is the differentiator: before/after operating metrics from a rhythm or automation you installed, with a benchmark and someone who could confirm it.
- The two-page limit is tight; strong submissions put the diagrams and artifacts (which do not count against it) to work and keep the prose dense with labeled specifics.

Strong or close submissions may be asked to run the triage live against a messy sample queue.
